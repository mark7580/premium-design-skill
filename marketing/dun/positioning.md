# DUN — Positioning & Category Strategy

> Working doc, v1 (June 2026). Source: founder strategy session "Dun marketing 1".

## 1. The category decision

DUN is **not** a vertical product. The incumbents all picked verticals and got
trapped in them:

| Company | Became |
|---|---|
| Thumbtack | "Find a contractor" |
| Angi | "Home services" |
| TaskRabbit | "Small tasks" |

DUN owns a **horizontal behavior** instead:

> **"I need something done."**

That behavior covers a roof quote, a wedding photographer, a tree removal, a
CPA, a golf instructor, a tow truck, a DJ, a website build. These are all the
same intent: the user is trying to move from **Need → Decision → Action**.
That journey — questions becoming actions — is the category DUN should own.

## 2. The real competitor

The biggest competitor is not Thumbtack. It's **Google** — and increasingly
ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity — because that's where "How much does
X cost?" and "Who can do X?" actually start today. The problem with those
tools is that the answer usually ends there.

DUN's wedge: **the place where questions become actions.**

## 3. How to describe DUN (and how not to)

**Never:**
- ❌ Service Marketplace
- ❌ Contractor Network
- ❌ Lead Generation Platform
- ❌ AI Quoting Tool

**Always (investor-facing one-liner):**

> DUN is an AI-powered service coordination platform that helps consumers
> discover costs, find professionals, compare options, and get real-world
> services completed through a single conversational interface.

## 4. Landing page direction

### Keep
The headline **"Get Anything Done."** — it's working.

### Fix
The current supporting copy ("DUN helps you find, compare, schedule, and
manage services…") is functional and immediately narrows perception back to
a marketplace. Replace it with emotional copy that implies providers, quotes,
and scheduling without naming them:

**Option A** (recommended — rhythmic, intent-mirroring)
> Need a price.
> Need a professional.
> Need something handled.
>
> Just ask.

**Option B**
> Life comes with questions.
>
> DUN helps you turn them into action.

**Option C**
> From quick answers to completed projects.
>
> DUN helps you move from wondering to done.

None of these mention providers, quotes, contractors, or marketplaces — yet
all are implied.

### The single biggest visual change
Stop showing plumbing. Show the **diversity of requests**. The moment a
visitor sees DUN handle a wedding photographer, a golf instructor, a tow
truck, an accountant, *and* a plumber in one interface, they stop comparing
it to Thumbtack and start seeing a new category. Example requests to rotate
through hero/demo surfaces:

- "How much does a new roof cost?"
- "I need a wedding photographer."
- "Can someone remove this tree?"
- "Find me a CPA."
- "I need a golf instructor."
- "My car broke down."
- "I need a DJ."
- "Can someone build my website?"

## 5. The future-vision section (the long game)

Today: *find someone*. Tomorrow: *manage your entire service life* —

- Saved providers and preferred vendors
- Service, home, and vehicle history
- Quotes, receipts, maintenance schedules
- AI recommendations

At that point DUN is less like Uber and more like **the operating system for
real-world services** — a category almost no one is pursuing. The landing
page should plant this idea now, even though the MVP launches much smaller.
